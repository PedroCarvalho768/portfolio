# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Developers, tech recruiters, and potential freelance clients visiting Pedro Carvalho's portfolio. They're technical people evaluating his backend work — they want to see competence, taste, and personality in one glance. The portfolio is his digital business card for the dev world.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that demonstrates Pedro's backend engineering skill through the design itself — the site should feel as sharp, direct, and opinionated as his code. Success is a visitor thinking "this person knows what they're doing" and reaching out for work.

## Brand Personality

Obscuro, Direto, Brutal. Dark-gothic newspaper aesthetic. Not "elegant dark mode" — think heavy ink, blackletter display type, hard borders, deliberate grit. The tone is confident and unsentimental: no fluff, no marketing speak, no decorative anything. Every element earns its place through weight and purpose.

## Anti-references

- Clean/minimalist white-space-heavy design (Apple-like, corporate minimal)
- SaaS gradients, glassmorphism, rounded cards
- Generic "dev dark theme" (terminal green, matrix tropes, hacker clichés)
- Anything that looks like a template or AI-generated

## Design Principles

1. **Weight as voice.** Typography is the primary emotional carrier. Blackletter for display, heavy serif for body — no light weights, no airy spacing. The page should feel physically substantial.

2. **One good idea per page.** Each section has a single typographic or layout gesture. No identical card grids, no repeated patterns. Every page invents its own shape.

3. **No decoration.** No gradients, no glass, no side-stripe accents, no icons that don't carry information. The ink-on-paper newspaper palette (paper, ink, one accent) is the entire system.

4. **Dark where it matters.** Not a dark mode — a dark register. Heavy borders, dense text blocks, blackletter display. The light paper bg creates contrast for the ink, not brightness.

5. **The code shows in the design.** A backend dev's portfolio should feel engineered — precise alignment, consistent rhythm, no gratuitous animation. Motion is functional (reveal on scroll), not decorative.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific requirements. Use common-sense contrast (body text ≥4.5:1 against bg), readable font sizes, and functional navigation. The dark-heavy aesthetic should not compromise legibility.
